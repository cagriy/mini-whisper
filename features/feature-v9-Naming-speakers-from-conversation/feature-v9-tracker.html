Detail

Summary

v8 names meeting speakers by voice, but only people already known; a new person stays "Speaker N" until renamed, and afterwards it is hard to remember who said what. v9 suggests names from what is said (introductions, hand-offs, a question to a name and its answer) with Claude Haiku 5.5. A request goes out after each turn that contains a name, sending the last X minutes chosen in Settings, or the whole meeting by default. One more request covers the whole meeting at Stop, and stored meetings get it on demand. In a replay on 17 stored meetings, 20 of 35 renamed speakers were shown the right name at some point, with a median of 4.2 minutes in.

In scope

  • A live request after each turn that contains a name.
  • Settings › Meetings: a switch, on by default and enabled with an Anthropic key; a slider from the last X minutes to "Whole meeting" (right end, default); a switch to apply verified self-introductions automatically, off by default.
  • Suggestions in v8's display with the quoted line they came from. They persist until you act, a different name may replace one, and they fill gaps only.
  • State passed forward: names suggested, accepted and dismissed; your name and its learned misspellings; Settings › People names.
  • "That's me": records a misheard spelling of your name across meetings.
  • Accepting a suggestion counts as naming the speaker, so v8 learns the voice.
  • A Stop pass over the whole meeting, and "Suggest names" on stored meetings.

Out of scope / deferred

  • Naming only at Stop; People names as engine vocabulary hints; a name-now hotkey; calendar attendees; suggestions to merge speakers; an MCP or CLI write tool; test-call scoring of names; auto-applying anything other than verified self-introductions.

Technical direction

  • Haiku 5.5 through the existing raw-HTTP Anthropic client; MiniWhisperCore stays dependency-free; the key only in the Keychain.
  • The transcript is data, never instructions. A suggestion counts only when its quote appears verbatim in the cited turn and contains the name.
  • Requests never block or interrupt recording; a failure costs only a suggestion.
  • All four meeting engines label speakers live. Suggestions fit v8's NameMatch with a non-voice source.
  • Costs bill to the meeting's cost line. The Settings footnote states that the transcript and People names are sent during the meeting.

Summary

Unnamed meeting speakers get names suggested from what is said (introductions, hand-offs, a question to a name and its answer), using Claude Haiku 5.5 over the whole transcript so far. Suggestions show the quoted line they came from, persist until the user acts, and never override a name the user or voice recognition gave.

Goals and non-goals

  • At least 20 of 35 renamed evaluation speakers shown the right name, at most 1 wrong; median time-to-name around 4 minutes.
  • Every suggestion evidenced by a verified quote; the user's misheard name suggested at most once per spelling.
  • About 3 cents a meeting, on the existing cost line.
  • Not in scope: window slider, engine vocabulary hints, calendar names, merge suggestions, agent write access, naming in test calls, voice learning from auto-applied names.

Design

Components

  • MWNaming (pure): NameFold, NameSpotter (NLTagger plus known names), NamingTrigger, NamingTranscript (turns, 5-minute blocks), NamingPrompt (system, state block, JSON schema), EvidenceCheck, NameSanitiser, SuggestionPolicy.
  • MWTranscription: NameSuggesting protocol; AnthropicClient.suggestNames (Haiku 5.5, effort medium, 8000 max tokens, 60 s, structured output, cache breakpoints).
  • MWUsage: Haiku rates incl. cache read 0.1×, write 1.25×, long-prompt card; TokenUsage cache fields.
  • MWMeetingPipeline: MeetingNamingRoute (trigger, single flight, failures, pause); SpeakerRegistry conversation matches; controller wiring in add, ticker, Stop, open, suggestNames, thatsMe.

Data

  • NameMatch source "conversation" with evidence {segment, time, text} and kind; SpeakerLabel.rejectedNames; Billing.namingCostUSD; config keys meeting_naming_enabled, meeting_naming_auto_apply, meeting_user_name_aliases, meeting_naming_explained. All additive.

Flow

  • Live: a name-bearing segment arms; the next speaker or 20 s fires; one request in flight, coalesced follow-ups; the reply is verified against the snapshot and decided against current speakers.
  • Stop: wait for the live request, then a whole-meeting pass; the summary waits only when auto-apply is on.
  • Accept renames as typing would (merge, link and learn with voices on); Dismiss records the name per speaker; That's me stores a misheard spelling of your name; Not reverts an auto-applied name.

Security and privacy

  • Transcript is data, never instructions; schema-constrained output; verified quotes; names limited to letters, marks, spaces and ' . -; key from the Keychain only; logs carry counts only.

Testing

  • Unit tests per MWNaming component, registry, Codable, pricing, client request shape, route (VirtualClock), controller flows, app window and settings models; opt-in replay test against stored meetings.

Risks

  • Cost grows with meeting length: caching, single flight, cap below the long-rate threshold.
  • Wrong names accepted or auto-applied: certain-only verified quotes, auto-apply off by default, Not reverts.
  • More frequent data sharing: disclosed on the switch and in a one-time notice.

Rollout

Ships on by default but inert without an Anthropic key; one-time notice; CHANGELOG entry; replay test before release; rollback by switch.

Detail

Overview

v9 suggests speaker names from what is said in a meeting, using Claude Haiku 5.5 over the whole transcript so far: live after a name-bearing turn, once at Stop, and on demand on a stopped or stored meeting. The plan builds bottom-up in 18 stages: the pure MWNaming module first, then shared and additive changes, the pure naming pieces, the client, the registry, the route, the controller, and the app surfaces last. Naming stays inert in the app until Stage 18 passes the namer, after every disclosure surface exists.

Cross-cutting concerns

  • Security: escaped tagged blocks under a data rule; schema-constrained output filtered by the sanitiser and evidence check; the key read at call time and never logged; no sending before Stage 18.
  • Performance: spotter on the main actor per segment; rendering and decoding off the main actor from an immutable snapshot; single flight and one follow-up; two cache breakpoints; 400,000-character cap.
  • Observability: Log.naming with counts, tokens, durations, cost and status only, checked with CapturingLogSink.
  • Compatibility: every new field optional or defaulted; Billing encodes byte-identically at zero naming cost; shared signatures gain only defaulted parameters; intermediate states are inert, never broken.

Full swift test and app tests green; the opt-in replay on the 17 evaluation meetings shows at least 20 of 35 right with at most 1 wrong; a manual pass over the four accepted surfaces in light and dark mode, the narrow transcript, the cost line and Today/Month, and the switch-off and test-call cases.

Planning decisions taken

  • The app stays inert until Stage 18 passes the namer, after the R1 and R26 disclosures; the Suggest names pill lands in that same stage.
  • The controller emits .naming(.finished(...)) for every scope; the window shows none or failed only after a manual Suggest names (R11).
  • The coordinator keeps a stopped session alive while its Stop pass runs, then calls changed().
  • PolicyContext carries each segment's current speaker for the auto-apply check.
  • The trigger arms at the segment's end; a firing name-bearing segment re-arms it.
  • A live reply landing during Stop is applied; only switch-off or a closed meeting drops it.
  • Other errors map to .transport; the People reader excludes the user's entry; defaultName moves to SpeakerLabel; a new autoApplyLocked flag; key presence refreshes on stop, page entry and window key; log lines split between client and route.
