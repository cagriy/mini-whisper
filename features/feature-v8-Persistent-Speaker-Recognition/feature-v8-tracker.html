Detail

Summary

Meeting speakers are numbered afresh in every meeting, and a name given to "Speaker 2" is forgotten when the meeting ends; only the user is recognised, and only in remote calls. This feature makes Mini Whisper remember the people the user names and name them in later meetings. It has two opt-in routes, chosen by the meeting engine: Speechmatics voice identifiers under the two Speechmatics engines, and a bundled local voice model (WeSpeaker) under the two AssemblyAI engines. Recognised names appear as applied names or as suggestions, depending on how sure the match is, and the user stays in control through accept, dismiss and the existing rename.

In scope

  • Two toggles under Settings › Meetings › Engine, both off by default. Layout settled at mockup.
  • Recognition in remote and in-room meetings, including recognising the user in the room.
  • Learning on every rename while a toggle is on, during the meeting or after Stop.
  • Each meeting keeps its speakers' voice data (vectors or identifiers, never audio) until the meeting is deleted or the data is removed from the People list.
  • Local model: sure matches applied, less sure ones suggested, the rest "Speaker N".
  • Speechmatics: a person's first match is a suggestion; once accepted, later matches are applied.
  • A manual rename always wins. Unaccepted suggestions stay "Speaker N" in summaries, exports and MCP results.
  • One "Cautious to Eager" slider, and an Advanced section with the raw sliders.
  • A People list: view, rename, merge, forget one, forget everyone.
  • One store at ~/.config/mini-whisper/voices.json. A person is learned separately by each provider.
  • A local log of match scores and outcomes, recorded only.
  • A credits window listing WeSpeaker and Sparkle.
  • A suggestion display that a second source can feed.

Out of scope or deferred

  • Naming speakers from what is said: separate feature, v9.
  • Enrolling voices from existing meetings' audio.
  • Back-filling earlier meetings when a person is named later.
  • People carrying over between providers, and cross-checking Speechmatics matches with the local model.
  • Reading the active speaker's name from the call app's window.
  • Agents naming speakers through MCP; the server stays read-only.
  • A "Who is this?" review of recurring unnamed voices: future consideration.
  • Self-tuning thresholds: future development; the log is recorded from v8.
  • Recognition in test calls.

Technical direction

  • No new third-party dependency, macOS 14 target, arm64, Swift 6 strict concurrency, durations tested on a virtual clock, settings in config.json.
  • The local model is wespeaker_v2.mlmodelc, about 8 MB, bundled and loaded with Core ML directly.
  • Measured on macOS 26.6.1 with synthetic voices: loads in 0.1 to 0.6 s, 15 to 23 ms per call; same voice 0.88 to 0.92, different voices 0.08 to 0.66.
  • The model takes one 10-second clip per call with three speaker masks, not three clips as the research note says.
  • A short clip padded with silence does not separate voices; the window must be filled with the speaker's own speech.
  • Not yet run on macOS 14 or on real meeting audio.
  • Speechmatics returns no match score; speaker_sensitivity is its only tuning. At most 50 identifiers per session. Identifiers are tied to the model, account and project.
  • The voice store is a plain file beside config.json, chosen over an encrypted file, one file per person and the Keychain.

Summary

Mini Whisper remembers the people the user names in meetings and names them in later ones. A sure voice match is applied as a normal name; a weaker one is offered as a provisional name. Only what the user confirms is learned, and a People section forgets one person or everyone.

Goals and non-goals

  • A person named once is named or suggested later on the same route.
  • No unconfirmed name reaches a summary, export, chip, search result or agent.
  • Both routes off by default; with both off nothing changes.
  • No voice data leaves the Mac on the local route; no name is sent to Speechmatics.
  • Not in this version: naming from what is said (v9), enrolling from old audio, carrying people between routes, test-call recognition, macOS 14 verification.

Design

Data

  • config.json: two switches and six tuning values.
  • meeting.json: a speaker may carry person, matches and rejected; written only when set.
  • meetings/<id>/voices.json: a vector or identifier per provider key, written at Stop.
  • ~/.config/mini-whisper/voices.json: people, up to 8 local samples each, one Speechmatics identifier per tier.
  • ~/.config/mini-whisper/voice-matches.jsonl: every match and what the user did with it.

Matching

  • Local: each final segment with at least 1.5 s of speech gives vectors from a 10 s window looped with the speaker's own speech. A voice is checked once it has 15 s; a person's score is the best cosine against their samples.
  • Speechmatics: known people are enrolled under mw:<uuid> labels at each session open; a person's first match is a suggestion, later ones are applied once the user has confirmed them.
  • Recognition only touches speakers still called "Speaker N".

Failure and privacy

  • A model that fails to load disables the local switch with its reason; the meeting carries on.
  • A lost Speechmatics session leaves its speakers unlearnable; a refused identifier is dropped and one notice is shown.
  • Files are 0600; logs carry no names, vectors or identifiers; the CLI and MCP server cannot reach voice data.

Testing

Every pure type and actor has its own suite on fakes. The real model is exercised by an AppTests test from the app bundle and by a macos-15 CI job; Speechmatics enrolment by an opt-in integration test.

Risks

  • Defaults rest on 18 remote meetings of one user; in-room data is thin.
  • macOS 14 is unverified for the local model; failure degrades to a disabled switch.
  • Speechmatics gives no score and can name an unknown voice as a similar enrolled one; sensitivity 0.80 and first-match-is-a-suggestion limit it.
  • Sending speaker_sensitivity changes Speechmatics diarization while its switch is on.
  • Voice data about other people sits in plain files, as meeting audio already does.

Rollout

One release with both switches off. A macos-15 CI job checks the committed model. Rollback is turning the switches off; the new files are inert without them.

Cross-cutting concerns

  • Security — Speechmatics messages are the trust boundary (opaque labels and identifiers, anchored warning parse, nothing logged); voice data 0600 and never logged; CLI/MCP see neither store nor sidecar; the model is hash-checked; Forget lands before the app turns learning on.
  • Performance — one locked append per buffer per tick; embedding on the recogniser's queue; writes only on user actions and at Stop.
  • Observability — Log.voice with keys, scores and seconds; the match log as the durable record.
  • Compatibility — no migration; exact-bytes SpeakerLabel encoding; defaulted parameters bound every shared signature's blast radius.
